Set reverse DNS (PTR) records for your assigned addresses in the **rDNS** tab. Prepare the hostname's forward DNS first: its A or AAAA record should resolve to the corresponding VPS address.

## Open the rDNS tab

Sign in to the [EDBB Control Panel](https://my.edbb.com/clientarea.php), open **Services → My Services**, select your VPS and choose **rDNS**.

## Set or remove an IPv4 PTR

1. Find the row for your IPv4 address.
2. Replace the **PTR Record** value with your fully qualified hostname, such as `mail.example.com`.
3. Click the **checkmark (Set)** button to save.
4. To remove your custom PTR, use the **red cross (Unset)** button.

Review the value after saving, then check the public result as described below.

## Add an IPv6 PTR

The IPv6 row shows the beginning of your allocation, followed by a suffix field and a hostname field.

1. Enter the suffix for the IPv6 address you use. With an example prefix of `2001:db8:1234:5678:`, the suffix `:1` completes `2001:db8:1234:5678::1`.
2. Enter the matching fully qualified hostname in the next field.
3. Click **add**, then review the record shown in the table.

Use only an address within your assigned allocation. Find it under [Network](/vps-management/ip-address-information).

## Verify the result

Replace the examples with your own address and hostname:

```bash
dig -x 192.0.2.10 +short
dig A mail.example.com +short
dig -x 2001:db8:1234:5678::1 +short
dig AAAA mail.example.com +short
```

The reverse lookup should return the intended hostname, and the corresponding forward lookup should include the address. Public DNS caches can retain earlier answers until their TTL expires.

If the result is unexpected, check spelling, the saved panel value and the forward record. PTR records alone do not replace a mail server's SPF, DKIM or other configuration.
