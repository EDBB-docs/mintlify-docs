If EDBB contacts you about suspected abuse involving your service, review the notification and respond promptly. Our [Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)](/acceptable-use-policy) defines prohibited activities and their consequences.

To report suspected abuse to EDBB, see [Reporting abuse](/misc/reporting-abuse).

## What to do

1. **Review the notification.** Identify the affected service and reported activity. Follow the instructions and any deadline in the message.
2. **Investigate and take action.** Stop prohibited activity, address its cause and secure any compromised systems or accounts.
3. **Reply in the same email conversation.** Confirm when the issue is resolved. For spam complaints, include a brief description of the remediation taken, as required by our [Port 25 policy](/faq/port-25-smtp-rate-limit-policy).
4. **Tell us if you disagree.** If you believe the report is incorrect, explain why and include relevant evidence so our team can review it.

Keep your account contact details current and monitor the address used for abuse notifications.

## Common types of abuse complaints

| Category | Examples | Relevant policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spam | Unsolicited email sent from your VPS | [Port 25 policy](/faq/port-25-smtp-rate-limit-policy) |
| Phishing | Deceptive websites or pages used to steal credentials | [AUP: Malware or phishing websites](/acceptable-use-policy#malware-or-phishing-websites) |
| Malware | Malicious software, botnets or abuse originating from compromised systems | [Acceptable Use Policy](/acceptable-use-policy) |
| Network attacks | DDoS attacks, port scans or other harmful network activity | [AUP: DDoS or network attacks](/acceptable-use-policy#ddos-or-network-attacks) |
| Content and rights complaints | Illegal content or alleged copyright, trademark or privacy violations | [Acceptable Use Policy](/acceptable-use-policy) |

## Immediate protective action

We may take action before you reply to protect affected parties and our infrastructure. Restrictions, suspension or account termination depend on the incident and the AUP. A response deadline does not guarantee that a service will remain online.

## If other people use your VPS

You remain responsible for activity from your server, including traffic generated by application users or VPN users. Investigate the affected service, stop prohibited activity and explain the action taken when you reply. This responsibility applies even when someone else caused the problem.
